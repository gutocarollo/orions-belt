Diátaxis is, at its core, an approach to **quality** in documentation. But
"quality" is a word in danger of losing its meaning — something we all approve
of yet rarely describe rigorously. Diátaxis tightens this grasp by positing a
distinction between **functional quality** and **deep quality**.

## Functional quality — accuracy you can measure

Functional quality is the set of standards that documentation must meet to do
its job:

- **accuracy**
- **completeness**
- **consistency**
- **usefulness**
- **precision**

These are **independent of each other**:
- Documentation can be accurate without being complete.
- It can be complete but inaccurate and inconsistent.
- It can be accurate, complete, consistent — and also useless.

Attaining functional quality means meeting high, objectively-measurable
standards in multiple independent dimensions, consistently. It requires
discipline and attention to detail, and high levels of technical skill.

To make it harder, any failure on any one dimension is **readily apparent**
to the user.

## Deep quality — the qualities you must judge

Beyond functional standards lies a second order of quality:

- _feeling good to use_
- _having flow_
- _fitting to human needs_
- _being beautiful_
- _anticipating the user_

These cannot be checked or measured. They can still be **identified** when
we encounter them. They are characteristics of _deep quality_.

Unlike functional quality, characteristics of deep quality are **interdependent**.
_Having flow_ and _anticipating the user_ are aspects of each other; it's hard
to see how something could feel good to use without fitting to our needs.

We do not **measure** deep quality — we **judge** it.

## The hierarchy

| Functional quality | Deep quality |
|---|---|
| independent characteristics | interdependent characteristics |
| objective | subjective |
| measured against the world | assessed against the human |
| a condition of deep quality | conditional upon functional quality |
| aspects of constraint | aspects of liberation |

**Deep quality is conditional upon functional quality.** Documentation can be
accurate and complete and consistent without being truly excellent — but it
will never have deep quality without being accurate, complete and consistent.
No user will experience documentation as beautiful if it is inaccurate, or
enjoy how it anticipates their needs if it is inconsistent.

Functional quality, to the creator, feels like a **burden and a constraint** —
a test they might fail, work that the next product release will demand again.
Deep quality, by contrast, feels like **liberation** — the work of creativity
or taste. To attain functional quality we must _conform_; to attain deep
quality we must _invent_.

## Recognising deep quality — the clothing analogy

Consider clothing. Clothes must have _functional quality_: they must keep us
appropriately warm and dry and stand up to wear. You don't need to know much
about clothes to assess these. If water gets in or the clothing falls apart,
it lacks quality.

Other characteristics — hanging well, moving well, being expertly shaped —
cannot be measured. To recognise them you must have at least a basic eye for
clothing. These are its characteristics of _deep quality_.

But: even someone who can't recognise _what_ they are — who cannot say what
they are — can still recognise that the clothing is excellent, because **it
feels good to wear**. No expertise is required to realise that clothing fits
and moves with you well. _Your body knows it._

Same in documentation. Perhaps you need to be a connoisseur to recognise
_what_ makes some documentation excellent, but that's not necessary to be
able to realise _that_ it is. Good documentation **feels good**; you feel
pleasure and satisfaction when you use it — it feels like it fits and moves
with you.

The users of our documentation may or may not understand why it's good. They
might recognise only the more obvious aspects of functional quality, mistaking
those for the deeper excellence. That doesn't matter — it will feel good, or
not, and that's what's important.

But **we, as creators**, need a clear and effective understanding of what
makes documentation good. We must develop our sense of it so that we recognise
_what_ is good, as well as _that_ it is good. And we need to develop an
understanding of how people will _feel_ when they use it.

## Diátaxis and quality

Functional quality's obligations are met through conscientious observance of
the craft of documentation — solid skill, knowledge of the technical domain,
the ability to gather a complete terrain into a single, coherent, consistent
map.

> **Diátaxis cannot address functional quality in documentation.** It is
> concerned only with certain aspects of deep quality, some more than others.

### Diátaxis exposes lapses in functional quality (analytical role)

Although Diátaxis cannot _give_ us functional quality, it can **serve** it by
exposing lapses that were obscured before.

For example, applying Diátaxis to existing documentation often makes problems
suddenly apparent that were hidden. Aligning the architecture of reference
documentation with the architecture of the code it documents (a Diátaxis
recommendation) makes gaps in the documentation more clearly visible. Moving
explanatory verbiage out of a tutorial often highlights a section where the
reader has been left to work something out for themselves.

As far as functional quality goes, Diátaxis principles have only this
_analytical_ role.

### Diátaxis creates conditions for deep quality (creative role)

For deep quality, Diátaxis can do more.

- It helps documentation _fit user needs_ by describing documentation modes
  that are based on them; its categories exist as a response to needs.
- It serves _flow_ — preventing rhythm disruptions that occur when something
  cuts across the user's purpose (e.g., a digression into explanation
  interrupting a how-to guide).

### Understanding the limits

It's important to understand that Diátaxis can never be _all_ that is required
in the pursuit of deep quality. It can _help_ attain beauty in documentation's
overall form, but it doesn't by itself _make documentation beautiful_.

Diátaxis offers **principles, not a formula**. It cannot offer a short-cut
that bypasses UX, UI or visual design.

Using Diátaxis does not guarantee deep quality. The characteristics of deep
quality are forever being renegotiated, reinterpreted, rediscovered and
reinvented. What Diátaxis _can_ do is lay down some **conditions for the
possibility** of deep quality.

## Audit checklist

Use this when reviewing a documentation set against canonical Diátaxis quality.

**Functional quality (the must-haves):**

- [ ] **Accuracy** — every statement matches the current state of the product
- [ ] **Completeness** — within its type, the doc covers what is needed
- [ ] **Consistency** — same patterns, same terminology, same structure
  throughout
- [ ] **Usefulness** — the doc actually serves a real user need; nothing is
  there just because it could be documented
- [ ] **Precision** — terms are exact; no hand-waving where specificity is
  possible

**Deep quality (the should-feel-rights):**

- [ ] **Feels good to use** — readers report pleasure / lack of friction
- [ ] **Has flow** — pace and rhythm match how users actually work
- [ ] **Fits human needs** — organisation reflects user needs, not internal
  product structure
- [ ] **Is beautiful** — visual / typographic / linguistic care
- [ ] **Anticipates the user** — answers the next question before it is asked;
  has the tool ready before the user reaches for it

If functional quality is failing, no amount of Diátaxis work will rescue deep
quality. Fix functional first.

If functional quality is solid but the docs still feel awkward, Diátaxis is
likely to help — but UX, IA and writing craft do most of the rest.
